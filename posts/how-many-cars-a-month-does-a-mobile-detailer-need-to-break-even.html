How Many Cars a Month Does a Mobile Detailer Need to Break Even

November 17, 2026

A lot of new detailers price by feel and book whatever comes in, then wonder in month three why the account is empty. Break even is the number of cars you must do before you earn a dollar for yourself. Here is how to find it with plain math, using made up numbers you can swap for your own.

Add up the bills that come every month

Say your fixed costs are insurance at 130 dollars, the van payment at 380, your phone and booking software at 50, marketing at 100, and payments on your equipment at 140. Together that is 800 dollars a month. You owe it whether you detail ten cars or none.

Add a storage space or a lot if you pay for one. Leave out anything you only pay when you work, because that goes in the next step.

Find what each car leaves after its own costs

Say your average detail sells for 150 dollars. Chemicals and towels for that car cost 15, fuel to get there costs 12, and the card fee is 4. That is 31 dollars of cost, so each car leaves 119 dollars toward your fixed bills.

Divide 800 by 119 and you get 6.72, so 7 cars covers the bills. Six cars gives you 714 dollars to put against the bills, and you owe 800, so you are 86 dollars short. Seven cars covers the 800 and leaves 33 dollars, which is not a paycheck. It only keeps the lights on.

Add what you want to take home

Now add your pay. If you want 3,000 dollars a month for yourself, you need to cover 3,800 dollars. 3,800 divided by 119 is 31.93, so you need 32 cars. At four weeks a month that is 8 cars a week.

Check that against the days you can actually work. If your calendar only holds 6 cars a week, the answer is a higher price, not more hustle.

See what a discount does to the number

Say you drop the price to 120 to win a booking. The card fee falls to 3, so the car costs 30 and leaves 90. Now 3,800 divided by 90 is 42.2, which means 43 cars.

A 30 dollar discount added 11 cars to your month. You can see that cost before you offer it. Your costs will be different from mine, but the method is the same, so run your own numbers before you say yes to a low price.
