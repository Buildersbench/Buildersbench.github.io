How to Handle Rain Days on a Weekly Mowing Route

November 17, 2026

When rain cancels a mowing day, the lost day is not the real damage. The damage is the lawns that quietly fall off the schedule afterward. Here is a simple way to move a rain day and keep every client mowed, with a worked example.

What a rained out day really costs

Say you mow 40 lawns a week, 8 a day from Monday to Friday, and each lawn pays 45 dollars. One day is 8 times 45, or 360 dollars. That is 360 dollars out of a 1,800 dollar week.

Rain takes Tuesday. If those 8 lawns get pushed to sometime next week, you have 360 dollars of work that either gets done late or gets forgotten. If rain takes two days, that is 720 dollars. The forgetting costs more than the rain.

Spread the day, do not skip it

Say 10 lawns is the most you can do in a day. Move 2 of Tuesday's lawns to each of Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday, and put the last 2 on Saturday. Wednesday through Friday each run 10 lawns, and Saturday runs 2. All 8 get mowed.

The longest any client waits is 4 days past their normal day, which means 11 days between mows. A skipped week would have been 14. Use the same order every time so you are not deciding it standing in the driveway.

Write down every move

A route breaks when the moves live in your head. Next Tuesday those 8 lawns belong back on Tuesday, but if nobody wrote down which ones moved, some stay on Wednesday and a lawn gets missed. Then the client is the one who tells you.

Track three things for each lawn: its normal day, the day you actually mowed it, and whether that visit was a make up. At the end of the week count the visits. With 40 lawns the count should be 40. If it says 38, two lawns got missed, and that is 90 dollars of work.

Tell clients the rule before the rain

Set one rain rule and tell every client once, in writing. For example, a rained out lawn gets mowed within 4 days. A late mow you warned about is easier to explain than a silent one. Put the same rule on your quotes and invoices so it is never a surprise.
