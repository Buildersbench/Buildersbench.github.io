How to Quote an Airbnb Turnover Cleaning Without Undercharging

November 18, 2026

Cleaning an Airbnb between guests is not the same as a weekly house clean. You have a checkout deadline, beds to make, laundry to handle, and supplies to check, all before the next guest arrives. If you quote it like a regular house cleaning, you can end up doing a lot of extra work for the same money. Here is how to price a turnover properly and make sure the host knows exactly what they are paying for.

Start with what the host actually needs

Before giving a price, find out what happens between checkout and check-in. Does the host expect you to wash and replace the sheets? Are you restocking toilet paper, soap, coffee, and other guest supplies? Do they want beds made a particular way or photos sent after every clean?

These are separate jobs, even if they happen during the same visit. A host who supplies clean linens is giving you a different job from one who expects you to wash the sheets at home and bring them back.

Ask about the property size, number of bathrooms, number of beds, pets, and how often guests leave the place. Get photos if you cannot walk through it.

Work out the real cost of a turnover

Start with the time: cleaning, making beds, checking supplies, taking photos, and handling laundry. Do not count only the time spent scrubbing bathrooms and kitchens.

Then add supplies, travel, laundry costs, and overhead. Your own detergent and equipment belong in the price, and so does the trip home with the laundry.

A flat price works well for regular turnover work, but only when you know what the flat price covers. Anything outside that agreement should be priced separately.

A worked example for a two bedroom rental

Say a two bedroom rental takes three and a half hours to clean and reset. Your labor at 25 dollars an hour is 87.50 dollars. Laundry costs 16, supplies and restocking 12, travel 15, and your share of overhead 10.

Your total cost is 140.50 dollars.

If you want a 25 percent profit margin, divide 140.50 by 0.75. That gives you 187.33 dollars. You could quote 188 dollars per standard turnover.

That price covers the laundry, supplies, travel, and standard cleaning, nothing more. A deep clean, excessive mess, or extra services need their own prices.

Put the scope and price in writing

A verbal agreement is where problems start. The host remembers that you agreed to clean the property. You remember agreeing to clean it, change the beds, and check the supplies. Neither of you has the same definition of finished.

Write down exactly what the standard turnover includes: whether laundry is included, who supplies guest toiletries, and whether you send completion photos. Also explain what happens if the property is left unusually dirty or checkout runs late.

Give the host a clear price for the regular turnover and separate prices for optional extras. When your costs change, you have a written starting point for discussing a new rate.
