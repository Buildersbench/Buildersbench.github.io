What Minimum Service Charge Should a Handyman Set

November 17, 2026

The jobs that hurt a handyman are rarely the big ones. They are the quick ones, like hanging a shelf or swapping a faucet, where you bill thirty minutes and lose most of a morning. A minimum service charge fixes that, but only if you build it from the whole visit and not just the time with a tool in your hand. Here is the math.

Count the whole visit, not just the work

Say your rate is 75 dollars an hour and a customer wants one shelf hung. You spend 10 minutes going back and forth on the quote. You spend 15 minutes picking up anchors and brackets. You drive 20 minutes each way, and you are on site for 30. That is 95 minutes of your day, and that is a normal small call, not a bad one.

If you bill the 30 minutes on site, you charge 37.50 dollars. Spread over the 95 minutes you actually spent, that is about 23.68 dollars an hour. You told yourself 75.

Set the minimum from that visit

Turn the full 95 minutes into money at your rate. 95 minutes at 75 dollars an hour comes to 118.75 dollars. Round it to 120 and that is your minimum. Any job that prices out below 120 gets charged 120, with materials on top.

Say it plainly on the first phone call, before you drive anywhere. It is a lot easier to explain on the phone than at the front door with a shelf half hung. If a customer asks why, tell them it covers your drive, your time getting the parts, and holding the slot for their job.

Check the bigger jobs after you finish them

A minimum only protects you on small calls. Bigger flat price jobs can leak the same way. Say you quote 240 dollars for a repair, spend 62 dollars on materials, work 3 hours on site, and add an hour of driving and shopping.

After materials you have 178 dollars left for four hours of your time. That is 44.50 an hour against a 75 dollar rate, and you would never know it unless you wrote it down. Track the price, the materials, and the hours on every job. After a few weeks you can see which kinds of work pay and which ones need a higher quote.

When a kind of job keeps coming in under your rate, raise that quote or turn the job down. Either is better than doing it for 44.50 an hour on repeat.
