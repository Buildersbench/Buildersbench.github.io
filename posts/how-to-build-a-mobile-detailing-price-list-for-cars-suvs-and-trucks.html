How to Build a Mobile Detailing Price List for Cars, SUVs, and Trucks

November 18, 2026

If you run a mobile detailing business, customers want to know what a detail costs before they book. Giving everyone a different number over text gets old quickly. A clear price list lets customers see what they are getting and keeps you from charging the same amount for vehicles that take very different amounts of work.

Separate your services into packages

Decide what services you want to sell: an exterior wash, an interior detail, and a full detail that combines both.

Be specific about what each package includes. An interior detail might include vacuuming, wiping surfaces, cleaning the glass, and cleaning floor mats. A more involved package might add carpet extraction, seat shampooing, and stain treatment.

Do not promise every service in your standard package. Pet hair removal, heavy stain treatment, engine bay cleaning, and odor treatment can be separate extras, so basic cleaning customers are not paying for work they do not need.

Set different prices for different vehicle sizes

A compact sedan and a large SUV are not the same job. Larger vehicles have more carpet, more seats, and more glass.

Work out how long each vehicle category takes you from your own completed jobs, rather than guessing from how big the vehicle looks.

For example, suppose your interior package costs you 75 dollars on a sedan, including labor, supplies, travel, and overhead. If you want a 25 percent profit margin, divide 75 by 0.75. Your selling price needs to be 100 dollars.

Now suppose a larger SUV costs you 105 dollars to complete. Dividing 105 by 0.75 gives you a selling price of 140 dollars.

The difference is based on the cost of the work, not an arbitrary surcharge.

Price extra work separately

Vehicle condition can change the job as much as vehicle size. A clean interior is different from one covered in pet hair, spilled drinks, and mud.

Ask about the vehicle before confirming the appointment, and request photos when they mention stains, odors, or heavy pet hair.

Suppose removing heavy pet hair adds an hour of labor and 5 dollars in supplies. At 30 dollars an hour, that extra work costs you 35 dollars.

At a 25 percent profit margin, divide 35 by 0.75. You need to charge 46.67 dollars to cover the extra work and hit that margin, so quote 47 as a separate service.

Agree on the extra price before starting. Do not discover halfway through that you promised hours of extra work for free.

Make your price list easy to quote from

Once your packages, vehicle categories, and extras are worked out, keep them where you can reach them when customers call. Use the same descriptions and pricing on your website, social media, and estimates.

Your quote should name the package, the vehicle category, and any additional services. Also state what is not included, so there is no confusion when you arrive.

Review your prices when your costs change. If a package keeps taking longer than expected, update the price instead of taking lower profit on every appointment.
