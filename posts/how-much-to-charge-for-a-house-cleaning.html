How Much to Charge for a House Cleaning

November 17, 2026

Most cleaners set their price by checking what the company across town charges. That tells you what customers will pay, but it does not tell you whether you make money at that number. Here is how to work out your own price from your own costs, with math you can copy.

Start with what a clean really costs you

Every clean has four costs. There is your labor, your supplies, your drive, and a slice of your overhead, which covers things like insurance, your phone, and your software. Add those four together and you have the lowest price you can charge without losing money.

Most owners count the hours inside the house and the supplies, and forget the other two. The half hour in the car and the monthly insurance bill are why a job that looks fine on paper ends up paying you nothing. Once you have the total, add a cushion on top, because you are in this to earn, not to break even.

A worked price for a three bedroom house

Say the house takes you three hours and you pay yourself 25 dollars an hour. That is 75 dollars of labor. Supplies run 6 dollars. The drive, counting your time and your gas, comes to 15 dollars. Your overhead works out to 9 dollars a job. Together that is 105 dollars.

Now add a 30 percent cushion. 105 times 1.30 is 136.50 dollars, so that is your price. If the cleaner across town charges 110 and you match it, you keep 5 dollars after paying yourself for three hours and covering every cost. At 136.50 you keep 31.50. Same house, same work. The cushion is not greed. It pays for the slow month, the supply price jump, and the client who cancels the morning of.

Set a minimum so small jobs do not cost you

A one bedroom apartment takes you 1.5 hours. Labor is 37.50 dollars and supplies are 4. The drive and overhead stay at 15 and 9, because the car ride does not get shorter for a small place. That puts your cost at 65.50 dollars.

Quote it at 50 dollars to be nice and you lose 15.50 on the job. Run the same cushion, 65.50 times 1.30, and you get 85.15, so a minimum of 85 dollars makes sense for these numbers.

Your numbers will differ from mine. Put in your own pay rate, drive, and overhead and the method holds. Do it once for your usual house sizes and you stop guessing at the door. Write the minimum down where you can see it, so you quote the same number on a bad day as you do on a good one.
